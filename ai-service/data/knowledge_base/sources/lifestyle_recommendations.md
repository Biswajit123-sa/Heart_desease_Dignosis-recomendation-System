# Lifestyle and Preventive Recommendations for Heart Health

## Diet
A heart-healthy diet emphasizes vegetables, fruits, whole grains, lean
protein, and healthy fats (such as those found in olive oil, nuts, and
fish), while limiting saturated fat, added sugar, and sodium. The
Mediterranean and DASH dietary patterns are commonly recommended for
reducing cardiovascular risk.

## Physical Activity
Adults are generally encouraged to aim for at least 150 minutes per week
of moderate-intensity aerobic activity (such as brisk walking), combined
with muscle-strengthening activity on two or more days per week, subject
to individual medical clearance.

## Smoking Cessation
Quitting smoking is one of the most effective single steps a person can
take to reduce cardiovascular risk. Risk begins to decline within weeks
of quitting and continues to fall over subsequent years.

## Weight Management
Maintaining a healthy body weight through diet and exercise reduces
strain on the heart and improves blood pressure, cholesterol, and blood
sugar control.

## Blood Pressure and Cholesterol Monitoring
Regular monitoring allows early detection of hypertension or unfavorable
lipid levels, enabling timely lifestyle changes or medical treatment
before complications develop.

## Diabetes Management
For individuals with diabetes, maintaining blood sugar within target
ranges through diet, exercise, and medication as prescribed helps protect
blood vessels and reduce cardiovascular complications.

## Stress Management and Sleep
Chronic stress and poor sleep are associated with elevated blood pressure
and unhealthy coping behaviors. Adequate sleep and stress-reduction
practices (such as relaxation techniques or regular physical activity)
support overall cardiovascular health.

## Regular Checkups
Routine checkups allow healthcare providers to assess risk factors,
order appropriate screening tests, and adjust prevention or treatment
plans over time.
